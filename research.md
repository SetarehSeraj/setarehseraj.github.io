**When Universities Go Online: Employment Consequences of IT Master's Program Adoption for Bachelor's Graduates**

Setareh Seraj, Shuting (Ada) Wang, Chengxin Cao & Yuxiao Luo · *Job Market Paper — in preparation for submission to MIS Quarterly*

More than half of U.S. graduate students now take at least one course online,
and universities have raced to launch online IT master's programs. This paper
asks what that expansion means for the students already on campus: how does a
university's decision to open an online IT master's program affect the
employment outcomes of its own bachelor's graduates? Drawing on signaling and
brand-extension theory, we develop competing predictions — online expansion can
dilute the institutional signal and crowd the entry-level market, or raise
visibility and modernize the institution's brand — and examine how
institutional ranking and local labor-market conditions determine which force
prevails.

**Economic Volatility and the Dynamics of Online Reading Behavior**

Setareh Seraj · *Under review at International Journal of Information Management*

Conference presentations: CIST 2025 (Atlanta) · WISE 2025 (Nashville)

Using large-scale user-level data from an online reading platform, this study
examines how economic volatility reshapes a digital behavior that demands
sustained attention. When volatility spikes, users spend more time reading and
shift toward educational rather than entertainment content, consistent with
coping and self-improvement motives — yet their ability to stay focused
declines, reflecting cognitive load and attentional strain. The findings
position online reading platforms as coping tools during periods of economic
uncertainty.

**An Empirical Investigation of Green Brand Reputation Using Social Media**

Setareh Seraj · *Under review at Information Systems Education Journal*

Most brand-reputation measures rely on periodic surveys and miss the
environmental dimension of reputation entirely. Building on green brand equity
theory, this paper develops a real-time tracker that measures green brand
image, green satisfaction, and green trust from social media text using
theory-driven, expert-reviewed dictionaries. Applied to monthly Twitter data
for firms in the Clean200 sustainability ranking, the tracker shows that
stakeholder perception often diverges sharply from sustainability-revenue
rankings — firms with high clean revenue, such as Apple and Tesla, are not
necessarily perceived as green.
